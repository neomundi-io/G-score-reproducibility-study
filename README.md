# NeoMundi — Mission : Reproducibility and Invariance of the G-score

## Overview

This repository contains the data, analysis notebook, visualizations, and reports produced as part of **NeoMundi Mission 2**.

The study focuses on the **reproducibility, stability, and invariance of the G-score** across different models, prompts, and measurement campaigns (barometers).

The objective is not to demonstrate that the score is invariant, but to **test this hypothesis using the available data** and identify the conditions under which G-score behavior remains stable or changes.

---

## Research question

> **To what extent does the G-score exhibit stable and reproducible behavior when repetitions, models, prompts, and measurement campaigns vary?**

The study aims to:

- characterize the distribution and dispersion of the G-score;
- assess the reproducibility of repeated runs of the same prompt;
- measure variability between prompts and between models;
- compare G-score levels across measurement campaigns;
- characterize the break observed in **B5**;
- examine the evolution of the score after B5, particularly in **B7 and B8**;
- investigate relationships between the G-score and available execution variables;
- assess the robustness of the results to atypical observations;
- evaluate the extent to which the results support or challenge the invariance hypothesis.

The invariance hypothesis is therefore treated as a **research hypothesis to be evaluated**, rather than as an expected result.

## Data

The theoretical corpus contains:

| Dimension | Number |
|---|---:|
| Campaigns / barometers | 7 |
| Models | 12 |
| Prompts | 4 |
| Repetitions per model × barometer × prompt | 100 |
| Model × barometer combinations | 84 |
| Theoretical observations | **33,600** |

The analyzed campaigns are:

**B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, B7, and B8.**

Barometer B6 is not available in the corpus.

Each model × barometer × prompt combination theoretically contains 100 repetitions, resulting in 400 observations per model × barometer combination.

The consolidated dataset contains **19 variables**, covering in particular:

- model, barometer, and prompt identifiers;
- repetition index;
- G-score and related scores;
- decision (`ALLOW`, `FLAG`);
- latency;
- token count;
- technical errors.

### Quality control

Several checks are performed before the statistical analyses:

- coverage of model × barometer combinations;
- 400 observations per model × barometer combination;
- 100 repetitions per prompt;
- absence of experimental duplicates;
- absence of exact duplicates;
- missing-value checks;
- G-score range checks;
- consistency between technical errors and missing G-score values.

Of the 33,600 theoretical observations, **199 contain a technical execution error** and therefore do not provide an exploitable G-score. Analyses of the G-score use the valid observations.

---

## Reference file selection

Some model × barometer combinations are associated with several result files.

When this occurs, the notebook selects a reference file using a deterministic rule:

1. **minimum number of technical errors**;
2. in case of a tie, **maximum number of complete rows**.

This selection is recorded in a **selection log** so that the construction of the analysis corpus remains traceable.

A sensitivity analysis was also performed on the different attempts available for B5. The choice of the reference file does not account for the magnitude of the break observed in B5.

## Statistical methods

The methods were selected according to the repeated structure of the data and the empirical properties of the G-score.

### Descriptive statistics

- mean;
- median;
- standard deviation;
- quartiles;
- minimum and maximum values;
- 95% confidence intervals obtained by bootstrap.

### Reproducibility

- coefficient of variation (**CV**);
- intraclass correlation coefficient **ICC(1,1)**;
- autocorrelation between successive repetitions;
- decomposition of within-prompt and between-prompt variability.

### Model comparison

- **Kruskal–Wallis** test;
- post-hoc **Dunn** tests;
- **Holm** correction for multiple comparisons;
- **Cliff's delta** as an effect-size measure.

### B5 break analysis

The B5 campaign is compared with the B1–B4 reference period using:

- **Mann–Whitney** test;
- Cliff's delta;
- bootstrap confidence interval;
- linear mixed-effects model;
- **model × period** interaction.

### B7–B8 follow-up

B7 and B8 are analyzed as follow-up campaigns to determine whether the change observed in B5 persists or whether the score returns toward reference levels.

A mixed-effects model is also used to assess whether the post-B5 evolution differs across models.

### Execution variables

Associations between the G-score and available quantitative execution variables are assessed using **Spearman correlation**, particularly for:

- latency;
- token count.

These analyses describe statistical associations and do not, by themselves, establish causal relationships.

### Robustness and atypical observations

Atypical observations are identified using the **IQR rule**.

They are not automatically removed. Their influence is examined through a sensitivity analysis to determine whether the conclusions are mainly driven by a small number of extreme observations or reflect the broader structure of the corpus.

## Main results

### 1. Stability during the reference period

Across **B1–B4**, the G-score shows a very stable central level:

- mean ranging from **0.5973 to 0.5989**;
- median equal to **0.600** in all four campaigns.

No systematic temporal drift is identified across the repeated runs.

The variability observed during the reference period is mainly within-prompt: **98.38%** of the total variability is attributed to the within-prompt component, compared with **1.62%** to the between-prompt component.

This stability in the mean level does not imply perfect reproducibility of every individual repetition: dispersion varies across configurations.

### 2. Differences between models

Statistically detectable differences are observed between some models during the reference period.

However, the effect sizes associated with the significant post-hoc comparisons remain **negligible**. Statistical significance should therefore be distinguished from practical importance.

### 3. Break in B5

The main change occurs in **B5**:

- B1–B4 reference mean: **0.5982**;
- B5 mean: **0.5266**;
- difference: **−0.0716**.

The decrease is observed across all models, but its magnitude varies between models.

The mixed-effects model shows a highly significant model × period interaction:

```text
χ² = 361.729
df = 11
p = 7.973 × 10⁻⁷¹
```

This indicates that the magnitude of the change between the reference period and B5 is not homogeneous across models.

### 4. Return toward reference levels

After B5, the score returns toward levels close to those observed during the reference period:

- B7: **0.5984**;
- B8: **0.6000**.

The recovery is not identical across all models. The model × period interaction for the reference → B7 → B8 trajectory is also significant:

```text
χ² = 821.187
df = 22
p = 1.848 × 10⁻¹⁵⁹
```

The results are therefore consistent with a mainly **transient break**, followed by a return toward the reference level, with recovery magnitude varying across models.

### 5. Execution variables

The overall correlation between G-score and latency is positive:

```text
ρ = 0.5662
```

but this association disappears when periods are considered separately:

```text
B1–B4 : ρ = −0.0011
B5    : ρ = −0.0169
```

The relationship with token count is also very weak:

```text
All campaigns : ρ = −0.0302
B1–B4         : ρ = −0.0362
B5            : ρ = −0.0226
```

These results therefore provide no evidence for a simple explanation of the B5 break based solely on latency or token count.

### 6. Atypical observations

The IQR rule identifies **4,965 atypical observations out of 33,401 valid observations**, including **4,653 in B5**.

This strong concentration indicates that the rule mainly identifies observations belonging to the particular distribution observed in B5, rather than a small number of isolated anomalies.

The observations are therefore retained in the main analysis and their influence is examined separately.

---

## Scientific conclusion

The results do not support **strict invariance** of the G-score across all conditions studied.

Instead, they point to **conditional stability**:

- the G-score level is stable across the B1–B4 reference campaigns;
- reproducibility varies across configurations;
- a clear break occurs in B5;
- the score subsequently returns toward reference levels in B7 and B8;
- the magnitude of both the break and the recovery varies across models.

The available data do not make it possible to identify the cause of the B5 break with certainty. Analyses of the available execution variables do not reveal a simple explanation based solely on latency, token count, or technical errors.

Within the scope of this study, the G-score is therefore interpreted as a **stability/behavior signal of the studied measurement system**, rather than as a direct measure of factuality or answer quality.

## Limitations

The main limitations of the study are:

- only four prompts per model × barometer combination;
- repeated observations that are therefore not fully independent;
- sensitivity of some reproducibility indicators, particularly ICC, to the small number of prompts;
- limited discrimination of the IQR rule when the quartiles are concentrated at 0.600;
- some analyses remain exploratory;
- the available variables do not allow causal identification of the B5 break.

A future study could increase the number of prompts, models, repetitions, and campaigns, and document more precisely the experimental conditions potentially associated with regime changes.
